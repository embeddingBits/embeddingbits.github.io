Making the switch to Emacs

Switching from Neovim to Emacs

January 27, 2026   ·  2 min read  ·  255 words

#Note_taking #Text Editor #Emacs #Org Mode

Switching to Emacs

I recently switched from Neovim to Emacs. I initially only wanted to try Emacs and I saw myself rolling my own config and to be an actual switch to Emacs. Emacs was compelling enough to replace my default editor entirely.

The most significant shift was in note taking. I moved away from Obsidian to Emacs Org mode. With Org-roam, my notes gained structure without losing simplicity. The keybinds were more configurable than in Obsidian and gave me a greater control. This completed the FOSSification of my note-taking. I've always wanted to try out org mode for a long time, but I've been too lazy try out for some reason.

Emacs also reduced my reliance on external tools. Previously, I depended on separate applications for specific tasks. Zathura was my primary PDF viewer. Now, pdf-tools inside Emacs handles that role. Search, annotations, and document links stay in one environment. This reduced constant window switching which I felt were easier to easier on focus and on the eyes.

Fewer external dependencies also mean fewer chances for the system to fail. This consolidation aligns naturally with long-term FOSS thinking. Emacs feels less like an editor. It feels more like a programmable knowledge system. Neovim was efficient and sharp. Emacs feels infrastructural and expansive.

Thanks to my friend @zstg for helping me with his valuable recommendations.
