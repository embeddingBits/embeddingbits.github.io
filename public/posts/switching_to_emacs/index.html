Switching to Emacs

I recently switched from Neovim to Emacs. I initially only wanted to try Emacs but turned out to be an actual switch to Emacs. Emacs was compelling enough to replace my default editor entirely.

The most significant shift was in note taking. I moved away from Obsidian to pure Emacs Org mode. With Org-roam, my notes gained structure without losing simplicity. This completed the FOSSification of my note-taking. They feel durable in a way proprietary formats never did.

Emacs also reduced my reliance on external tools. Previously, I depended on separate applications for specific tasks. Zathura was my primary PDF viewer. Now, pdf-tools inside Emacs handles that role. Search, annotations, and document links stay in one environment. This reduced constant window switching which I felt were easier to the change was easier on focus and on the eyes.

Fewer external dependencies also mean fewer chances for the system to fail. This consolidation aligns naturally with long-term FOSS thinking. Emacs feels less like an editor. It feels more like a programmable knowledge system. Neovim was efficient and sharp. Emacs feels infrastructural and expansive.

I am grateful to my friend @zstg for helping me with his valuable recommendations.
